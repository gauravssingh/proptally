00 · The pitch

Rental property finances, kept like books.

Most independent landlords do not have a math problem. They have a structure problem: the spreadsheet they inherited counts cash, not what the cash means. A mortgage payment is principal, interest, and escrow at once. One hardware-store receipt belongs to three doors and a personal bathroom. In April, the year has to be rebuilt from memory. PropTally replaces that spreadsheet with a double-entry ledger built for residential rentals, so every reported figure is posted, traceable, and defensible.

The problem we are solving

  • Cash counting hides the economics. A mortgage payment is not an expense; a credit-card payment is not a second expense; escrow is not free. Tools that record only inflows and outflows flatten all three and the numbers stop tying out.
  • One transaction, many doors. Mixed-use cards, shared maintenance, and personal draws make “which property paid for this?” a monthly argument. Manual percentage guesses turn into unreconcilable books.
  • Institutional software, consumer data terms. Property-management suites assume dozens of units and a bank feed. Cloud bookkeeping asks you to upload deeds, leases, mortgage statements, and tax workpapers to somebody else’s server.
  • Tax season becomes archaeology. Schedule E asks for income and expenses by category. Reconstructing that from a year of receipts is where weekends and CPA fees disappear.

Existing tools each solve a slice: spreadsheets are flexible but ungoverned, consumer budgeting apps are single-entry, and enterprise property management is priced and designed for portfolios a small landlord will never have. Nobody built the middle — until now.

The elevator pitch

Your portfolio’s books, not another budgeting app.

PropTally is a self-hosted, double-entry financial system for independent landlords with one to ten doors. It turns rent, mortgages, escrow, and a folder of receipts into auditable per-door books and a Schedule E workpaper — without sending the documents anywhere.

In one line: the rigor of a general ledger, the scope of a small landlord, and the privacy of a filing cabinet.

Why it holds up

01

Double-entry, to the cent

Every entry is a balanced journal transaction in integer cents. Posted entries are immutable at the database level; corrections are audited compensating reversals, never quiet edits.

02

Mortgages modeled honestly

Statement receipt and bank settlement are separate stages, so the loan balance reflects what the servicer actually applied. No phantom equity from counting a payment twice — or from counting it too early.

03

Exact splits across doors

One outflow can be allocated across rental doors and personal use with absolute-value validation. The books say who paid for what, and the reports agree.

04

Evidence, not guesses

Documents are extracted locally and every proposed value cites its page and supporting text. Nothing posts until a person approves it. AI extraction is optional, off by default, and restricted to a local or private endpoint.

05

Reports you can trace

NOI, operating cash flow, debt service, CADS, per-door P&L, and the cash-flow waterfall are computed from posted entries — not from a parallel spreadsheet — and the Schedule E workpaper exports to CSV.

06

Private by architecture

Each portfolio gets its own database and document vault on your infrastructure. There is no bank feed, no cloud OCR, and no third-party analytics; the data does not leave the host.

Beta ground rules

  • Keep an independent copy. Retain your source statements and take a portfolio export regularly; do not make PropTally your only record.
  • Do not file from it unreviewed. The tax workpapers are an aid, not a filed return, and not tax, legal, or accounting advice.
  • Do not work around a ledger error. If a balance, allocation, or report looks wrong, stop and report it rather than entering a correction that hides it.
  • Only enter data you are authorized to hold. This is a shared beta; treat every uploaded statement and document accordingly.

02 · Orientation

What PropTally is — and is not

PropTally records the money that moves around a small residential portfolio and turns it into books you can inspect. It is built around a double-entry ledger rather than a spreadsheet, so every reported figure comes from posted journal entries and can be traced back to a statement or a manual entry.

At a glance
Area What it gives you
Portfolio Doors, ownership type, valuation, cost basis, photos, and per-property operating statements.
Ledger Rent, operating expenses, mortgage payments, escrow, transfers, and exact splits across doors and personal use.
Reconciliation Bank, card, and mortgage statements matched to the right account, with a review queue for anything unresolved.
Reporting Equity, debt, NOI, OCF, CADS, a cash-flow waterfall, per-door P&L, and a Schedule E workpaper with CSV export.
Documents A per-portfolio vault for statements, leases, deeds, policies, and receipts, stored once by content hash.
Access Four portfolio roles, invitations, a separate operator console, and physical separation between portfolios.
×

What it is not

  • Not a bank or payment processor. It does not collect rent, move money, or act as a tenant portal.
  • Not a bank-feed product. Statements are uploaded or dropped into a watched folder; there is no live bank connection.
  • Not a property-management CRM. It is a financial ledger and tax workpaper tool, not leasing, maintenance, or tenant communication software.
  • Not your CPA. It prepares workpapers for review; it does not verify a CPA’s identity or file a return.

The short version

One private bookshelf per portfolio.

Each portfolio has its own database and document vault. You open one portfolio at a time, and the figures on screen belong only to it.

Designed for

Independent investors and small operators with roughly 1–10 residential doors, including a primary residence alongside rental properties.

03 · Getting in

Signup: from invitation to an empty portfolio

The beta is invitation-based. Whoever runs your PropTally instance creates a single-use onboarding code and sends you either a link or the code itself. One code starts exactly one portfolio, expires after 14 days, and can be withdrawn.

In this guide, operator means the person who runs the instance and manages accounts, invitations, and backups. An account is your identity; a portfolio is the workspace. Redeeming a code creates both together.

01

Receive your invitation

A link looks like /auth/join?code=…. If you receive a code instead, you will enter it on the registration page. If the invitation is pinned to an email address, that address is fixed; otherwise use the address you want to sign in with.

02

Create your account and name the portfolio

Give your name, a portfolio name, your email, and a password of at least 10 characters. A link carries the code for you; on /auth/register you will also see a field for the code when the instance requires one.

03

Land in an empty portfolio

Redeeming the code creates the account, the portfolio, and your OWNER membership. There is no sample data: the portfolio starts with the standard chart of accounts and generic vendor rules, and nothing else.

04

Choose how to start

The empty portfolio offers two paths: enter the essentials manually, or upload a ZIP of existing documents and review what PropTally proposes. Both paths end in the same guided setup and the same dashboard.

Have ready before you start

  • Your invitation link or code
  • A portfolio name you recognize
  • The email you will sign in with
  • A password of 10+ characters
  • For the document path: deeds, mortgage statements, bank/card statements, leases, tax and insurance documents

A ZIP that is already organized by property or servicer is easier to reconcile, but folders are not required.

i

Registration rules, without the guesswork

  • First owner on a fresh host. If the instance has no portfolio yet, the first owner can register at /auth/register without a code. Once the instance is claimed, registration requires an invitation code.
  • Already have an account? Sign in at /auth/login; do not redeem a new portfolio code with the same account.
  • Joining somebody else’s portfolio? The owner invites you from Administration → People → Invite somebody, chooses your role, and gives you the generated link. The link is shown once, expires after 7 days, and holds no access until you accept it.
  • No email is sent. Invitations, password resets, and onboarding codes are delivered out of band by the person who runs the instance.
If signup does not go as expected

04 · First setup

Two ways to start: type it in, or bring your documents

A new portfolio is empty on purpose. You can build the essentials one step at a time, or hand PropTally a ZIP of documents and review every proposed record before it is written. The two paths meet in the same guided setup.

Path A

Set up manually

Best when your records are in your head, in a spreadsheet, or spread across a few documents you would rather type in.

  1. Property. The door the portfolio reports on: address, type, acquisition date, valuation, and cost basis.
  2. Bank or card account. Where rent lands and expenses are paid; classify it as dedicated rental, mixed use, or unclassified.
  3. Mortgage (optional). Servicer, loan number, rate, term, payment, and escrow. Skip it if the property is free and clear.
  4. Opening balances. One dated baseline for cost basis, cash, card balances, loan principal, and escrow.
  5. First records (recommended). Upload a statement or enter a first transaction. This step is not required to publish.

The first four steps are the publishing gate. The wizard saves as you go and resumes at the first unfinished step, so you can leave and come back.

Path B

Import existing documents

Best when you already have the paperwork. One ZIP goes in; a review queue of proposed properties, accounts, mortgages, and leases comes out.

  • One archive. Up to 50 MB, 200 files, 25 MB per file, 250 MB extracted.
  • Accepted inside. PDF, PNG, JPG, TIFF, DOCX, XLSX, CSV, and TXT. Unsafe paths and executable content are rejected.
  • Background processing. One job per document; you can close the browser and come back.
  • Reconciliation. Related documents are grouped, disagreements become conflicts, and every proposed value cites its document and page.
  • Review. Accept, edit, reject, or defer each record. Required fields and unresolved conflicts block approval.
  • Provisioning. Only approved records are created, through the same setup services as the manual path.

Abandoned import sessions expire after 30 days. Approved source documents are copied into the portfolio’s own vault.

✓

The rule that matters: nothing is written until you approve it

Document import proposes; you decide. A proposal is not a ledger entry, an accepted proposal is not a posted transaction, and an imported property or loan does not invent opening balances. After import, the guided setup resumes at whatever is still missing.

!

What the document path can and cannot create

The import can create properties, financial accounts, loan accounts, and leases. Bank statements are retained and linked to the portfolio, but bank statement transactions are not posted to the ledger by the onboarding import. Use Statements & Ingestion for transaction import and the review queue for allocation and posting. Rental, tax, insurance, valuation, and maintenance documents are classified and retained; they cite evidence rather than becoming separate records.

05 · The mental model

How the app operates

Most of PropTally’s behavior follows from six ideas. Once these are clear, the screens are easier to read and the numbers are easier to trust — or challenge.

01

One portfolio, one private database

Each portfolio has its own SQLite database and its own vault under the instance’s storage. A signed-in session resolves to one portfolio at a time; another portfolio’s ledger is not reachable from inside it.

02

Doors are the unit of reporting

Every property is a door. Rental doors carry operating metrics and Schedule E lines; a primary residence is visually separated and excluded from rental income and depreciation.

03

Reports read posted entries, not raw activity

A transaction can be imported, split, and allocated without yet affecting the reports. Only a posted journal entry does. That is why the dashboard can show zero until statements are reviewed and posted.

04

Double-entry, integer cents, immutable once posted

Debits must equal credits, and amounts are stored as integer cents. Posted journal entries and lines cannot be edited or deleted; corrections are compensating entries, so history stays intact.

05

Documents are evidence

Statements and source documents live in a content-addressed vault, deduplicated by SHA-256. An extracted figure can point back to the document and page that produced it, and the original file is never renamed on disk.

06

Roles decide authority

Four portfolio roles separate administration, bookkeeping, external review, and read-only access. The app enforces the same role sets in its routes and in its People screen, so the screen cannot describe authority the app will not carry out.

Reading the headline numbers

The dashboard’s vocabulary
Figure What it means Source and caveat
Portfolio equity Recorded valuation less total mortgage debt across the portfolio. Valuation is manual or appraised input, not a live market quote. Treat it as an estimate.
Total debt outstanding Principal still owed across all doors and servicers. Servicer-reported principal is authoritative where a statement exists; otherwise the posted-ledger principal is used.
NOI Rental income less operating expenses, before debt service. Rental-only and unleveraged. Personal draws and primary-residence costs are excluded.
OCF Cash the operating property produces before debt service. Cash-backed, derived from posted entries. It is not the same as accounting profit.
CADS Cash retained after operating expenses and full debt service. The “what is left in the operating account” figure. Principal paydown builds equity; it is not an expense.
Escrow reserve Money held by the servicer for taxes and insurance. An asset, not operating cash. Calculated and statement-reported balances are shown separately.
i

Why a credit-card payment is not a second expense

The original purchase already carried the expense. Paying the card from a deposit account is a transfer: it debits the card liability and credits the bank. When you record it, choose the card as the transfer target. Only the payment’s allocated rental share should reduce rental cash flow; the purchase is not expensed twice.

5.1

Portfolio Overview

Find it: Portfolio Overview in the sidebar

  • Four headline KPIs: equity, debt, rental NOI, and CADS.
  • Door cards that separate rental doors from the primary residence.
  • A cash-flow waterfall from gross rent through operating expenses, interest, escrow, principal, and retained cash.
  • An Action required panel that points at the review queue when something is waiting.

Good to know: every figure is posted-entry only. A portfolio that has imported statements but not posted transactions will show very little here, by design.

5.2

Doors: property detail

Find it: Portfolio doors → a property

  • Address, type, acquisition date, valuation, cost basis, occupancy, and photos.
  • Operating P&L: monthly income and Schedule E expense categories from posted entries.
  • Mortgage debt and clearing ledger: principal, interest, escrow, and in-flight obligations.
  • Tenant and lease: tenant name, term, monthly rent, security deposit, and whether the signed lease is in the vault.

Good to know: a tenant inferred from rent-like bank deposits is labeled unverified; it is not proof of a signed lease. A primary residence is a non-operating asset and is excluded from rental metrics.

5.3

Mortgages & Escrow

Find it: Money → Mortgages & Escrow

  • Consolidated loan ledger across servicers, with masked loan numbers and rates.
  • Ledger principal, servicer-reported principal, and clearing balance kept in separate columns with their provenance.
  • Escrow reserve accounts showing calculated and statement-reported balances.
  • In-flight obligations that are recorded but not yet confirmed as cash-settled and applied by the servicer.

Good to know: escrow is an asset held by the servicer, not cash available to spend. Two bases are shown so one never masquerades as the other.

5.4

Transactions Register

Find it: Money → Transactions Register

  • Filter by account, property, status, flow direction, merchant, date, and source.
  • Add a transaction manually, including a credit-card payment as a transfer.
  • Split one outflow across multiple doors, categories, and a personal share.
  • Assign a category and post the allocation to the ledger when it is complete.

Good to know: a split must allocate every cent; the save control stays blocked until the split sum equals the transaction total. Posted entries are locked and cannot be re-split.

5.5

Review Queue

Find it: Money → Review Queue

  • One queue for unallocated activity, with counts for the whole queue and the current filter.
  • Vendor rules map repeat merchants to a service type and default Schedule E category; apply one suggestion or batch-classify.
  • Manual assignment for anything a rule cannot explain.
  • Separate governance controls for CBD-1 (client confirmation) and CBD-2 (external review of a mixed-use card charge).

Good to know: rules are suggestions, not silent postings. CBD-1 and CBD-2 are not interchangeable: a client sign-off does not satisfy a review gate.

5.6

Statements & Ingestion

Find it: Records → Statements & Ingestion

  • Upload a statement PDF up to 50 MB; it is stored in the vault and matched against the portfolio’s registered accounts.
  • Review the extracted figures, correct them, and re-validate.
  • Approve records the decision but writes nothing to the ledger. Import makes the statement the authoritative record for that account and period and creates its itemized transactions.
  • An unrecognized account can be registered directly from the statement.

Good to know: importing a statement creates transactions for allocation; it does not post them. Importing the same statement twice is refused.

5.7

Document Vault

Find it: Records → Document Vault

  • Browse by property and document type, with Unfiled and Unclassified folders for the rest.
  • Search across every folder; the folder tree’s counts follow the search.
  • Upload several PDF, PNG, JPG, or WEBP files at once, up to 50 MB each.
  • Preview, download, and open the related extraction when one exists.

Good to know: files are stored by SHA-256, not by original filename. Re-uploading known content does not duplicate it, and an existing classification is never overwritten.

5.8

Tax & Schedule E

Find it: Tax → Tax & Schedule E

  • Choose a tax year and read one column per rental door, with a portfolio total.
  • Lines 3–21 map posted income and expense categories to the IRS Schedule E form.
  • Depreciation uses the building basis, 27.5-year straight line, and the mid-month convention in the acquisition year.
  • Export the workpaper as CSV for a CPA or tax-prep software.

Good to know: unposted splits are excluded and called out as provisional. If land/building basis is missing, Line 18 cannot be computed; the workpaper says so rather than guessing.

5.9

Manage Portfolio Data

Find it: Settings → Manage Portfolio Data

  • Add or edit properties, bank and card accounts, mortgage loans, and vendor rules.
  • Record the dated opening balance baseline for the portfolio.
  • Revisit setup after publishing; the wizard is a view over the same write services as this screen.

Good to know: opening balances are ledger history, not a scratch pad. Change them deliberately, and ask your operator for a backup first if you are unsure.

5.10

Administration

Find it: Settings → Administration

  • People: members, roles, a capability matrix, invitations, withdraw, and remove.
  • Portfolio status: counts of doors, loans, documents, transactions, journal entries, and files, plus database readability and storage sizes.
  • Backup: an owner-only download of the portfolio database, vault, photos, and a manifest.
  • Sign-in: your identity, role, and password change.

Good to know: the last owner cannot be demoted or removed. Invitation links are shown once because the app sends no email.

5.11

All Portfolios

Find it: Portfolios → All Portfolios, when the account reaches more than one

  • One cross-portfolio view listing each portfolio, your role, door count, net equity, and debt.
  • Open or switch the active workspace; a new sign-in with multiple memberships presents a chooser.
  • Owners can export or remove a portfolio from here. An export contains one portfolio’s database, documents, photos, and manifest — never another portfolio and never the control plane.

Good to know: a single-portfolio account does not see this screen; the workspace switcher already expresses the same choice.

!

Statement parser coverage is deliberately narrow in beta

The built-in deterministic parsers currently target Rocket, PNC, and CrossCountry mortgage statements, Bank of America checking and credit-card statements, and Apple Card. Other layouts may be stored and marked unsupported or sent to review rather than guessed at. A scanned PDF needs OCR on the host; if OCR is unavailable, the document fails with a reason instead of appearing successfully empty.

07 · Operating rhythm

A month with PropTally

The healthy loop is short: capture what happened, match it to the right account, allocate every cent, post it to the ledger, then read the reports that result.

Capture → Match → Allocate → Post → Review
A practical cadence
When Do this Why it matters
Day one Create the portfolio, add the first door, account, mortgage (or skip), and opening balances. Publish, then add a first statement or manual transaction. Gives every later figure a door, an account, and a dated baseline.
Each statement cycle Upload the statement, review the extracted figures, approve, then import. In the review queue, allocate and post the transactions. Statements are evidence; the ledger is the result. Allocation is what makes the expense belong to a door.
Weekly Clear the review queue, check unmatched activity, and upload any new statements. A small queue is easy to reason about; a large one hides anomalies.
Monthly Reconcile bank, card, and mortgage statements; check escrow balances; read NOI and CADS; scan for uncategorized or personal items. Catches double-counting, missing payments, and misallocated expenses before they reach tax season.
Quarter-end Review per-door P&L and large repairs or capital items with your CPA; confirm the depreciation basis is recorded. Keeps tax treatment decisions out of the year-end crunch.
Year-end Pick the tax year in Tax & Schedule E, review the workpaper, export the CSV, and download a portfolio export. Gives your CPA a traceable workpaper and gives you an independent copy of the books.
When life changes Add a door, account, or loan in Manage Portfolio Data; update lease terms; file the signed lease in the vault. The setup wizard is a starting path, not a one-time cage. The portfolio keeps growing.
✓

A good month looks boring

Every statement is imported, every transaction is allocated, the review queue is empty, escrow matches the servicer, and the only open question is a real one for your CPA. Boring is the goal.

08 · People

Roles, invitations, and access

Roles are portfolio-level and enforced by the application, not just described on a screen. An owner can invite people from Administration → People; the invitation link is shown once and expires after 7 days.

Portfolio roles
Role Can Cannot
OWNER Administer people and invitations, do setup and bookkeeping, confirm for the client, export the portfolio, and remove it. —
MANAGER Do setup and bookkeeping: import statements, allocate and post transactions, and manage properties, accounts, and loans. Manage people, provide the client’s confirmation, export, or remove the portfolio.
CPA_REVIEWER Do bookkeeping as needed and record a verified external review. Manage people or provide the client’s own confirmation.
READ_ONLY Read portfolio data. Change or export it.

Inviting somebody

Administration → People → Invite somebody. Choose the role, then copy the generated link. No email is sent; the link is the delivery mechanism.

Removing access

An owner can change a role or remove a person. The last owner cannot be demoted or removed, so a portfolio cannot be left without an administrator.

External review in beta

A CPA_REVIEWER membership is recorded as a verified reviewer. However, the current CBD-2 posting path also accepts an owner’s attestation checkbox. Treat that checkbox as a process note, not as independent CPA sign-off.

Reporting a problem

Send it to the person who invited you to the beta. Include:

  • Date and time, and the portfolio you were in
  • The screen name and URL
  • What you did, step by step
  • What you expected and what happened
  • A screenshot, with account numbers and personal data redacted
  • Your browser and whether the issue reproduces

Report immediately if…

  • A ledger balance or report disagrees with a statement in a way you cannot explain.
  • An import is stuck, duplicated, or created the wrong entity.
  • You can see another portfolio’s data, or someone else can see yours.
  • A password, invitation, or session behaves unexpectedly.
  • You suspect data was lost after an upgrade or restart.

Do not try to repair a ledger discrepancy by posting a balancing entry. Preserve the evidence and let the operator inspect it first.
